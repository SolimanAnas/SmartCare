# Chapter 4 – Cardiovascular & Resuscitation: reference notes

## 4.1 Anatomy and physiology

- Four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle.
  The left ventricle has the thickest wall (pumps against systemic
  resistance). (NEMSES)
- Valves: tricuspid (RA–RV), pulmonic (RV–pulmonary artery), mitral (LA–LV),
  aortic (LV–aorta). AV valves: tricuspid and mitral; semilunar: pulmonic and
  aortic. S1 = AV valves closing; S2 = semilunar valves closing. (NEMSES)
- Coronary arteries: LAD – anterior wall and septum; circumflex – lateral
  wall; right coronary artery – right ventricle, inferior wall, and in most
  people the SA and AV nodes (inferior MI → bradycardia, heart block).
  (NEMSES)
- Coronary arteries fill mainly during diastole; very fast rates shorten
  diastole and can cause ischemia.
- Conduction: SA node (intrinsic rate 60–100/min) → AV node (delay lets the
  atria empty: "atrial kick") → bundle of His → bundle branches → Purkinje
  fibres. Escape rates: AV junction 40–60/min; ventricles 20–40/min.
  (NEMSES)
- Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume; blood pressure = cardiac output
  × systemic vascular resistance. Stroke volume depends on preload,
  contractility and afterload. (NEMSES)
- Sympathetic (beta-1) stimulation raises rate, contractility and conduction
  speed; the vagus nerve slows the rate. (NEMSES)
- Aortic dissection: a difference of more than 20 mmHg in systolic pressure
  between arms, or a pulse deficit, supports the diagnosis. (ACC/AHA 2022
  aortic disease guideline; NEMSES)

## 4.2 Chest pain and acute coronary syndrome

- ACS: unstable angina, NSTEMI, STEMI (complete occlusion → ST elevation).
  (4th UDMI; ACC/AHA 2025 ACS)
- 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of first medical contact for chest pain or
  other symptoms of ACS; transmit and pre-notify. (ACC/AHA 2025 ACS)
- Aspirin 162–325 mg, non-enteric, chewed, unless truly allergic or actively
  bleeding. (ACC/AHA 2025 ACS)
- Nitroglycerin 0.4 mg sublingual every 5 minutes, up to 3 doses, for
  ongoing ischemic pain. Do not give if systolic BP below 90 mmHg, heart rate
  below 50/min, suspected right ventricular infarction, or a PDE-5 inhibitor
  taken recently: sildenafil or vardenafil within 24 hours, tadalafil within
  48 hours. (ACC/AHA 2025 ACS; ESC 2023 ACS)
- Oxygen only when SpO₂ is below 90% or the patient is short of breath;
  routine oxygen does not help normoxic patients. (ACC/AHA 2025 ACS;
  AVOID, DETO2X trials)
- STEMI: transport directly to a PCI-capable centre; target first medical
  contact to device 90 minutes or less. If PCI cannot be done within 120
  minutes, fibrinolysis within 30 minutes of arrival. (ACC/AHA 2025 ACS;
  ESC 2023 ACS)
- Inferior STEMI: record right-sided leads (V4R); RV infarction is preload
  dependent – avoid nitrates, give fluid for hypotension per protocol.
  (ESC 2023 ACS)
- Atypical presentations (no chest pain: breathlessness, nausea, fatigue,
  epigastric pain) are commoner in women, older adults and people with
  diabetes. (ACC/AHA 2025 ACS)
- OPQRST: onset, provocation/palliation, quality, region/radiation, severity,
  time.
- Non-modifiable risk factors: age (men 45 or older, women 55 or older),
  male sex, family history of premature coronary disease. (AHA)
- Other life threats with chest pain: aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism,
  tension pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade, esophageal rupture.

## 4.3 Heart failure and pulmonary edema

- Left heart failure → pulmonary congestion: breathlessness, orthopnea,
  paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, crackles, pink frothy sputum. Right heart
  failure → systemic congestion: raised JVP, peripheral edema, liver
  congestion. (ESC 2021 HF)
- Acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema: sit upright, oxygen to SpO₂ 94–98%,
  CPAP (typical start 5–10 cmH₂O), nitrates if systolic BP is adequate
  (90 mmHg or more, or your protocol's threshold); loop diuretic per protocol.
  (ESC 2021 HF; NEMSES)
- CPAP not for: unresponsive, cannot protect airway, systolic BP below 90 mmHg,
  vomiting, suspected pneumothorax. (NEMSES)
- Hypotension with pulmonary edema = cardiogenic shock: no nitrates; ventilate
  if failing; vasopressors per protocol (ALS). (ESC 2021 HF)
- Triggers: stopped medication, too much salt or fluid, ischemia, arrhythmia
  (especially atrial fibrillation), infection, uncontrolled hypertension,
  renal failure, pulmonary embolism. (ESC 2021 HF)
- "Cardiac asthma": wheeze from bronchial wall edema in heart failure.
- Furosemide acts by diuresis and early venodilation; slower than CPAP and
  nitrates.

## 4.4 Hypertensive emergencies

- Severe hypertension: systolic above 180 and/or diastolic above 120 mmHg.
  Emergency = with acute target-organ damage; urgency = without.
  (ACC/AHA 2017 HTN)
- Target organs: brain (encephalopathy, stroke), heart (ACS, pulmonary edema),
  aorta (dissection), kidneys, eyes, pregnancy (preeclampsia).
- EMS does not lower blood pressure for the number; treat the organ problem
  and transport. (NEMSES)
- Suspected aortic dissection: tearing pain to the back, arm pressure
  difference above 20 mmHg, pulse deficit; withhold aspirin if dissection is
  suspected; rapid transport to a centre with cardiothoracic surgery.
- Preeclampsia: new BP 140/90 or more after 20 weeks of pregnancy with
  proteinuria or organ involvement; severe features include BP 160/110 or
  more, severe headache, visual disturbance, right upper quadrant pain,
  pulmonary edema. Can present up to 6 weeks after delivery. Eclampsia =
  seizures. Magnesium sulfate per protocol (ALS). (ACOG)
- Left lateral position in late pregnancy; dim lights, minimise stimulation.
- Cushing's triad: hypertension (widening pulse pressure), bradycardia,
  irregular respirations = raised intracranial pressure; do not lower BP.
- Rebound hypertension after stopping clonidine or beta-blockers.
- Head of the stretcher raised 30° for suspected raised intracranial
  pressure. Ventilate 10 breaths/min to EtCO₂ 35–45 mmHg; brief
  hyperventilation only for signs of herniation, per protocol. (BTF)

## 4.5 Dysrhythmia recognition

- Read every strip in order: rate, rhythm, P waves, PR interval, QRS width.
  Then ask: is the patient stable? Unstable = hypotension, altered mental
  status, signs of shock, ischemic chest pain, acute heart failure.
  (AHA 2025)
- Sinus bradycardia below 60/min; sinus tachycardia above 100/min.
- Symptomatic bradycardia: atropine 1 mg IV every 3–5 minutes, maximum
  3 mg; if ineffective, transcutaneous pacing or an infusion of dopamine
  5–20 mcg/kg/min or epinephrine 2–10 mcg/min. Atropine often fails in
  Mobitz II and complete heart block with wide QRS. (AHA 2025)
- First-degree AV block: PR longer than 0.20 s. Mobitz I (Wenckebach):
  PR lengthens until a QRS drops. Mobitz II: fixed PR with dropped QRS,
  may progress – prepare to pace. Third-degree: P waves and QRS unrelated.
- Tachycardia with a pulse, unstable: synchronized cardioversion.
  Stable narrow regular (SVT): vagal manoeuvres (modified Valsalva), then
  adenosine 6 mg rapid IV push with a flush, then 12 mg. Stable wide
  regular: amiodarone 150 mg over 10 minutes or expert advice; treat as VT
  until proven otherwise, especially with heart disease. (AHA 2025)
- Atrial fibrillation: irregularly irregular, no P waves. Atrial flutter:
  sawtooth flutter waves in II, III, aVF.
- Narrow QRS less than 0.12 s; wide 0.12 s or more.
- Shockable in arrest: VF and pulseless VT. Not shockable: asystole, PEA.
  PEA = organised electrical activity without a pulse: CPR, epinephrine,
  find the cause (Hs and Ts). (AHA 2025)

## 4.6 ECG basics and the 12-lead

- Paper at 25 mm/s: small box 0.04 s, large box 0.20 s; calibration
  10 mm = 1 mV. Rate: QRS complexes in a 6-second strip × 10.
- Normal PR 0.12–0.20 s (3–5 small boxes; 4 small boxes = 0.16 s); normal QRS
  less than 0.12 s.
- Chest leads: V1 4th intercostal space right sternal border; V2 4th
  intercostal space left sternal border; V4 5th intercostal space
  mid-clavicular line; V3 between V2 and V4; V5 anterior axillary line and
  V6 mid-axillary line at the level of V4. V4R: V4 position on the right.
  V7–V9: posterior, at the level of V6.
- STEMI (new ST elevation at the J point in 2 contiguous leads): 1 mm in all
  leads except V2–V3, where it is 2 mm in men 40 or older, 2.5 mm in men
  under 40, 1.5 mm in women. Reciprocal ST depression supports it.
  (4th UDMI)
- Territories: inferior II, III, aVF (right coronary artery – watch for
  bradycardia and RV infarction); anterior/septal V1–V4 (LAD); lateral I,
  aVL, V5–V6 (circumflex); posterior: ST depression V1–V3, elevation in
  V7–V9 (0.5 mm is enough there). (4th UDMI; ESC 2023 ACS)
- LBBB or paced rhythm with symptoms of ongoing ischemia: manage like STEMI;
  Sgarbossa criteria help. de Winter T waves: LAD occlusion equivalent.
  Wellens' T waves: critical LAD stenosis – high risk, no stress test.
  (ESC 2023 ACS)
- STEMI mimics: pericarditis (diffuse concave ST elevation, PR depression,
  no reciprocal change except aVR), early repolarization, LVH, LBBB,
  hyperkalemia, Brugada pattern.
- Repeat the 12-lead when symptoms change; transmit to the receiving centre.

## 4.7 Cardiac arrest and high-quality CPR

- Recognise arrest: unresponsive and not breathing normally (gasping is
  arrest). Health-care providers check a pulse for no more than 10 seconds.
  (AHA 2025)
- Compressions: lower half of the sternum, 100–120/min, depth at least 5 cm
  (2 inches) and not more than 6 cm (2.4 inches) – i.e. 5–6 cm, full recoil, pauses under
  10 seconds; switch compressors every 2 minutes. (AHA 2025, ERC 2025)
- Chest compression fraction: at least 60%, aim for 80% or more.
  (AHA 2025; resuscitation quality programmes)
- Without an advanced airway: 30:2. With an advanced airway: 1 breath every 6
  seconds (10 breaths/min) with continuous compressions; each breath about 1
  second. (AHA 2025)
- EtCO₂ below 10 mmHg during CPR: poor compressions (or very poor prognosis
  if it persists); an abrupt rise to 35–45 mmHg or more suggests ROSC.
  (AHA 2025)
- Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO every 3–5 minutes: as soon as possible for
  non-shockable rhythms; after initial defibrillation attempts for shockable
  rhythms. Amiodarone 300 mg then 150 mg, or lidocaine 1–1.5 mg/kg then
  0.5–0.75 mg/kg, for shock-refractory VF/pVT. (AHA 2025)
- Reversible causes (Hs and Ts): hypovolemia, hypoxia, hydrogen ion
  (acidosis), hypo-/hyperkalemia, hypothermia; tension pneumothorax,
  tamponade, toxins, thrombosis (pulmonary, coronary). (AHA 2025)
- Chain of survival: early recognition and call for help, early CPR, rapid
  defibrillation, advanced resuscitation, post-cardiac arrest care, recovery.
  (AHA)
- Full recoil lets the heart refill; leaning reduces venous return and
  coronary perfusion. (AHA 2025)

## 4.8 AED and defibrillation

- Turn on the AED, attach pads to the bare chest, stand clear for analysis,
  shock if advised, then resume compressions immediately for 2 minutes
  without a pulse or rhythm check. (AHA 2025)
- Pads: anterolateral (right upper chest below the clavicle; left lower
  chest in the mid-axillary line) or anteroposterior. Remove medication
  patches, dry the chest, shave heavy hair, avoid placing over an implanted
  device. Move the patient out of standing water. Oxygen source away from the
  chest during the shock. (AHA 2025, ERC 2025)
- Adult energy: biphasic per manufacturer (typically 120–200 J; 200 J if
  unknown); monophasic 360 J. (AHA 2025)
- Children: first shock 2 J/kg, second 4 J/kg, later 4 J/kg or more (maximum
  10 J/kg or the adult dose). Under 8 years: pediatric pads or attenuator if
  available, otherwise a standard AED. Infants: manual defibrillator
  preferred, then attenuated AED, then standard AED. If pads could touch,
  use anteroposterior. (AHA 2025)
- Lone rescuer, unwitnessed child collapse: about 2 minutes of CPR before
  leaving to call and fetch an AED; witnessed sudden collapse: call and get
  the AED first. (AHA 2025)
- "Check electrodes": press firmly, dry, shave, replace pads.

## 4.9 Post-cardiac arrest (ROSC) care

- Confirm ROSC: palpable pulse and measurable BP; sustained rise in EtCO₂.
  High risk of re-arrest – keep the pads on.
- Ventilate 10 breaths/min (1 every 6 seconds); target EtCO₂ 35–45 mmHg;
  SpO₂ 92–98% (avoid hypoxia and hyperoxia). (AHA 2025)
- Systolic BP 90 mmHg or more and MAP 65 mmHg or more; fluid and
  vasopressors (epinephrine or norepinephrine infusion, ALS) per protocol.
  (AHA 2025)
- 12-lead ECG as soon as possible: STEMI → emergency coronary angiography at
  a PCI-capable centre. (AHA 2025)
- Check glucose; treat seizures; keep temperature controlled: prevent fever;
  hospital temperature control 32–37.5 °C. Do not give rapid infusions of
  cold IV fluid in the field. (AHA 2025)
- Transport to a cardiac arrest centre where available. (AHA 2025, ERC 2025)
- Hyperventilation raises intrathoracic pressure, lowers cardiac output and
  causes cerebral vasoconstriction.

## 4.10 Shock and hemorrhage

- Types: hypovolemic (including hemorrhagic), cardiogenic, distributive
  (septic, anaphylactic, neurogenic), obstructive (tension pneumothorax,
  tamponade, pulmonary embolism). (NEMSES)
- Early signs: anxiety, tachycardia (first measurable sign), pale cool clammy
  skin, delayed capillary refill, fast breathing. Hypotension is late.
- Hemorrhage classes (ATLS): class I up to 15% loss; class II 15–30%
  (tachycardia, narrowed pulse pressure, BP maintained); class III 31–40%
  (hypotension, confusion, marked tachycardia); class IV more than 40%.
- Life-threatening limb bleeding: direct pressure, then a tourniquet 5–7 cm
  (2–3 inches) above the wound, not over a joint; tighten until bleeding
  stops; note the time; a second tourniquet above the first if needed.
  Junctional wounds: pack with hemostatic gauze and press. (ATLS; Stop the
  Bleed; TCCC)
- Suspected pelvic fracture with shock: pelvic binder at the level of the
  greater trochanters.
- Tranexamic acid within 3 hours of injury: 1 g IV over 10 minutes, per
  protocol (ALS). (CRASH-2)
- Keep warm: hypothermia, acidosis and coagulopathy worsen each other.
- Neurogenic shock: hypotension with bradycardia and warm, dry skin below
  the injury.
- Anaphylaxis: IM epinephrine 0.01 mg/kg into the anterolateral thigh,
  maximum 0.5 mg (adult 0.5 mg); repeat after 5 minutes if needed. (WAO)
- Tamponade: Beck's triad – hypotension, raised JVP, muffled heart sounds.
  Tension pneumothorax: hypotension, absent breath sounds, distended neck
  veins, hypoxia – needle decompression (ALS).

## 4.11 Fluid resuscitation

- Isotonic crystalloid: 0.9% saline or a balanced solution (lactated Ringer's).
  Large volumes of saline cause hyperchloremic acidosis. (SSC 2021)
- Bolus volume = mL/kg × weight: 20 mL/kg × 70 kg = 1400 mL; 20 mL/kg ×
  80 kg = 1600 mL; 20 mL/kg × 16 kg = 320 mL.
- Hemorrhagic shock (no head injury): small boluses, about 250 mL, to a
  palpable radial pulse or systolic BP 80–90 mmHg (permissive hypotension);
  blood products where available. (ATLS; ERC 2025)
- Head injury: avoid hypotension; keep systolic BP at least 110 mmHg in most
  adults. (BTF)
- Sepsis with hypotension: at least 30 mL/kg crystalloid within the first 3
  hours. (SSC 2021)
- Children in shock: 10–20 mL/kg boluses, reassess after each. (AHA 2025
  PALS)
- Signs of response: better mental status, falling heart rate, rising BP,
  stronger pulses, warmer skin. Overload: new crackles, worsening breathing,
  rising JVP – stop.
- Cardiogenic shock with pulmonary edema: no large boluses; small cautious
  challenge (250 mL) only if lungs are clear or per medical direction.
- IO access when IV access fails in critical patients.
- Warm the fluids; cold fluid worsens hypothermia.
- MAST/PASG no longer used: no survival benefit.

## 4.12 Pediatric cardiac arrest and BLS

- Most pediatric arrests follow respiratory failure and hypoxia. (AHA 2025)
- Infant: under 1 year. Child: 1 year to puberty.
- Compression to ventilation: one rescuer 30:2; two rescuers 15:2. With an
  advanced airway: 1 breath every 2–3 seconds (20–30 breaths/min) with
  continuous compressions. (AHA 2025)
- Depth at least one third of the chest depth: about 4 cm (1.5 inches) in
  infants and 5 cm (2 inches) in children; rate 100–120/min. (AHA 2025)
- Infants: two thumb-encircling hands or the heel of one hand; the
  two-finger technique is no longer recommended. Children: one or two hands.
  (AHA 2025)
- Heart rate below 60/min with poor perfusion despite oxygenation and
  ventilation: start CPR. (AHA 2025)
- Pulse: brachial (infant), carotid or femoral (child).
- Epinephrine 0.01 mg/kg IV/IO (0.1 mL/kg of 0.1 mg/mL), maximum 1 mg,
  every 3–5 minutes; amiodarone 5 mg/kg for shock-refractory VF/pVT.
  Shocks 2 J/kg then 4 J/kg. (AHA 2025)
- Length-based tape or drug calculator to avoid dosing errors.

## 4.13 Termination of resuscitation and verification of death

- Do not start resuscitation with obvious death: decapitation, decomposition,
  incineration, rigor mortis, dependent lividity, injuries incompatible with
  life; or a valid DNR / POLST / advance directive. (NAEMSP; AHA 2025)
- BLS termination rule: arrest not witnessed by EMS, no shock delivered, no
  ROSC → consider termination with medical direction. The ALS rule adds:
  not witnessed by a bystander and no bystander CPR. (AHA 2025; NAEMSP)
- EtCO₂ below 10 mmHg after 20 minutes of ALS is one factor, never the only
  one. (AHA 2025)
- Blunt trauma: pulseless, apneic, no organised rhythm on arrival → resuscitation
  can be withheld. (NAEMSP / ACS-COT)
- Special cases – resuscitate and seek advice: hypothermia, drowning,
  pregnancy, children, suspected poisoning.
- After death: tell the family clearly using "died", support them, record
  the time, preserve a possible crime scene, notify police or the coroner
  per local rules.

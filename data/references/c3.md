# Chapter 3 – Airway & Ventilation: reference notes

The facts the rewritten chapter relies on, each with its source. Every number
on a chapter 3 page or in a right answer must appear here
(`tests/test_content_guides.py`). Sources are international and current:

- **AHA 2025** – American Heart Association Guidelines for CPR and ECC (2025),
  Adult and Pediatric Basic and Advanced Life Support.
- **ERC 2025** – European Resuscitation Council Guidelines 2025.
- **BTS 2017** – British Thoracic Society guideline for oxygen use in adults
  in healthcare and emergency settings (O'Driscoll et al.), with the 2022 update.
- **NREMT / NEMSES** – US National EMS Education Standards and the National
  Registry's EMT and paramedic practice analysis.
- **PALS** – AHA Pediatric Advanced Life Support provider manual (2020/2025).

## 3.1 Anatomy and physiology

- The tongue is the most common cause of airway obstruction in an unresponsive
  supine patient; head-tilt/chin-lift or jaw thrust lifts it. (AHA 2025)
- Epiglottis covers the glottis during swallowing; vocal cords are the
  narrowest point of the adult airway; in young children the narrowest point
  is below the cords at the cricoid. (PALS)
- The right mainstem bronchus is shorter, wider and more vertical. (NEMSES)
- Gas exchange happens in the alveoli. Normal adult tidal volume is about
  500 mL. (NEMSES)
- Children: larger tongue and occiput, narrower airway, more compliant
  trachea; infants are preferential nose breathers. Pad under the shoulders of
  infants and small children to keep the airway neutral. (PALS)
- Normal respiratory rates (PALS): infant 30–53 breaths/min, toddler 22–37,
  preschool 20–28, school age 18–25, adolescent 12–20; adult 12–20 breaths/min.
  (AHA 2025)

## 3.2 Airway assessment

- Snoring: tongue / soft tissue → reposition, adjunct. Gurgling: fluid →
  suction. Stridor: upper airway narrowing (anaphylaxis, croup, epiglottitis,
  foreign body). Wheeze: lower airway bronchospasm. (NEMSES)
- Early hypoxia: restlessness, anxiety, tachycardia, tachypnea. Late: cyanosis,
  bradycardia (especially children), falling consciousness. (NEMSES, PALS)
- Speaking in 1–2 word sentences, tripod position, accessory muscles,
  retractions, nasal flaring, see-saw breathing = increased work of breathing.
- Normal SpO₂ is 94–98% on room air. Pulse oximetry is unreliable with poor
  perfusion, cold extremities and carbon monoxide poisoning (reads falsely
  normal). (BTS 2017)
- Normal end-tidal CO₂ is 35–45 mmHg. (AHA 2025)
- Respiratory patterns: Kussmaul (deep, rapid; metabolic acidosis, e.g. DKA),
  Cheyne-Stokes (crescendo–decrescendo with apnea; brain injury, heart
  failure), Biot / ataxic (irregular; brainstem injury), agonal (gasping;
  cardiac arrest – start CPR). (NEMSES)

## 3.3 Foreign body airway obstruction

- Mild obstruction (effective cough, can speak): encourage coughing, do not
  interfere, monitor. (AHA 2025, ERC 2025)
- Severe obstruction, responsive adult or child over 1 year: give 5 back
  blows, then 5 abdominal thrusts; repeat until relieved or unresponsive.
  (AHA 2025, ERC 2025)
- Pregnant (late pregnancy) or obese: chest thrusts instead of abdominal
  thrusts. (AHA 2025)
- Responsive infant under 1 year: 5 back blows then 5 chest thrusts with the
  heel of one hand on the lower half of the sternum (the two-finger technique
  is no longer recommended: it may not reach adequate depth); never abdominal
  thrusts. (AHA 2025)
- Becomes unresponsive: start CPR (compressions); each time the airway is
  opened, look in the mouth and remove an object only if seen. No blind finger
  sweeps. (AHA 2025)
- Anyone who had abdominal thrusts should be assessed for internal injury.
  (ERC 2025)
- Universal choking sign: hands clutching the throat.

## 3.4 Airway adjuncts

- OPA: unresponsive, no gag reflex. Size from the corner of the mouth to the
  angle of the jaw (earlobe). Adult insertion upside down then rotate 180°;
  children: insert right way up with a tongue depressor. (NEMSES)
- NPA: tolerated with a gag reflex. Size from the tip of the nose to the
  earlobe; lubricate; bevel toward the septum; larger nostril.
  Avoid with suspected basal skull fracture or severe midface trauma (raccoon
  eyes, Battle's sign, CSF from nose or ears). (NEMSES)
- Wrong size OPA: too long pushes the epiglottis over the glottis; too short
  pushes the tongue back.
- Adjuncts keep the airway open but do not protect against aspiration.

## 3.5 Oxygen therapy

- Target SpO₂ 94–98% for most acutely ill patients; 88–92% for patients at
  risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure (e.g. COPD). Do not withhold oxygen
  from a critically hypoxic COPD patient: treat, then titrate. (BTS 2017)
- Patients with SpO₂ in the normal range (94% or more) and no breathlessness –
  e.g. uncomplicated chest pain or stroke – do not need supplemental oxygen.
  (BTS 2017; AHA 2025 gives oxygen in ACS when SpO₂ is below 90%)
- Devices (approximate):
  - nasal cannula 1–6 L/min, about 24–44%;
  - simple face mask 6–10 L/min (at least 6 L/min to flush CO₂), about 40–60%;
  - non-rebreather mask 10–15 L/min, about 60–90%, reservoir must stay inflated;
  - bag-valve mask with reservoir at 15 L/min, close to 100%.
- Oxygen supports combustion; cylinders are high pressure: secure them, no
  smoking or open flame.

## 3.6 Ventilation

- Adult with a pulse, breathing absent or inadequate: 1 breath every 6
  seconds (10 breaths/min). (AHA 2025)
- Infant or child with a pulse: 1 breath every 2–3 seconds (20–30
  breaths/min). (AHA 2025, PALS)
- Each breath over about 1 second, just enough for visible chest rise
  (adult tidal volume about 500–600 mL, 6–7 mL/kg). (AHA 2025)
- Two-rescuer BVM (E-C clamp, two hands on the mask) gives a better seal than
  one rescuer. (AHA 2025)
- Oxygen at 15 L/min with the reservoir attached.
- Excessive rate or volume causes gastric distension, regurgitation,
  aspiration and raised intrathoracic pressure that lowers venous return and
  blood pressure. (AHA 2025)
- Routine cricoid pressure is not recommended. (AHA 2025)
- Poor chest rise: reposition, adjunct, two-rescuer seal, suction, check for
  obstruction.

## 3.7 Suction

- Rigid (Yankauer, tonsil tip) for the mouth and oropharynx and thick
  secretions; soft (French) catheter for the nose, through adjuncts and
  tubes. (NEMSES)
- Measure: corner of the mouth to the earlobe; do not insert beyond what you
  can see. Suction only while withdrawing. (NEMSES)
- Maximum per attempt: adult 15 seconds, child 10 seconds, infant 5 seconds.
  (NEMSES teaching standard)
- Suction removes oxygen too, and can cause hypoxia and vagal bradycardia,
  especially in infants and children: pre-oxygenate and re-oxygenate between
  passes; monitor heart rate and SpO₂. (PALS)
- Vomiting with no spinal concern: roll the patient onto the side.

## 3.8 Advanced airways

- Supraglottic airways (i-gel, King LT, LMA) sit above the vocal cords;
  inserted blind; fewer CPR interruptions. Endotracheal tubes pass through the
  cords. (AHA 2025)
- Waveform capnography is recommended to confirm and continuously monitor
  advanced airway placement. (AHA 2025)
- Also: bilateral breath sounds, no epigastric sounds, chest rise.
- After an advanced airway in cardiac arrest: 1 breath every 6 seconds
  (10 breaths/min) with continuous compressions. (AHA 2025)
- DOPE for deterioration after intubation: Displacement, Obstruction,
  Pneumothorax, Equipment failure. Sudden loss of the waveform = displacement
  until proven otherwise.
- Pre-oxygenate before any intubation attempt.

## 3.9 Respiratory distress and failure

- Distress: compensating (anxious, increased work of breathing, maintained
  SpO₂). Failure: decompensating (drowsy, tiring, slowing rate, falling SpO₂
  despite oxygen, silent chest) → assist ventilation now; do not wait for
  apnea. (PALS, NEMSES)
- Silent chest in asthma = minimal air movement, impending arrest. (BTS/SIGN
  asthma guideline)
- CPAP: conscious, breathing spontaneously, able to protect the airway, for
  moderate–severe distress (e.g. pulmonary edema, COPD); typical start 5–10
  cmH₂O. Contraindications include unresponsiveness, inability to protect the
  airway, hypotension (systolic below 90 mmHg), suspected pneumothorax,
  vomiting and facial trauma. (NEMSES)
- In children, bradycardia with respiratory distress is a pre-arrest sign:
  ventilate. (PALS)

# Chapter 9 – Emergency Pharmacology: reference notes

The facts the rewritten chapter relies on, each with its source. Every number
on a chapter 9 page or in a right answer must appear here
(`tests/test_content_guides.py`). Sources:

- **AHA 2025** – adult and pediatric advanced life support (epinephrine,
  amiodarone, adenosine, atropine, vascular access, ACS: aspirin,
  nitroglycerin, oxygen targets).
- **NASEMSO** – National Model EMS Clinical Guidelines (2022) and the US
  National EMS Scope of Practice Model.
- **AES 2016** – status epilepticus (midazolam 10 mg IM over 40 kg;
  intranasal 0.2 mg/kg, maximum 10 mg).
- **WAO / ERC** – anaphylaxis; **ADA** – hypoglycaemia (below 70 mg/dL,
  glucagon 0.5 mg under 25 kg).
- **BTS / GINA** – oxygen targets and asthma (albuterol).
- **SAMHSA / ASAM** – medications for opioid and alcohol use disorder;
  **ISMP / Joint Commission** – error-prone abbreviations, tall-man
  lettering, high-alert medications; **FDA** – removal of ratio labels for
  epinephrine; **DEA** – controlled substance schedules I–V.
- **Pharmacology texts** – half-life (steady state after about 4–5
  half-lives), first-pass effect, IM site volumes (vastus lateralis up to
  5 mL, ventrogluteal up to 3 mL, deltoid up to 1 mL in adults).

## Core doses (adult unless stated)

- Oxygen to SpO₂ 94–98%; 88–92% for COPD with CO₂ retention. (BTS, AHA)
- Aspirin 162–325 mg chewed (e.g. 324 mg = 4 × 81 mg). (AHA)
- Nitroglycerin 0.4 mg sublingual every 5 minutes, up to 3; not if SBP below
  90 mmHg, sildenafil or vardenafil within 24 hours, tadalafil within
  48 hours; caution in RV infarction. Sublingual onset 1–3 minutes. (AHA)
- Oral glucose 15 g, recheck in 15 minutes. Glucagon 1 mg IM (0.5 mg under
  25 kg), onset about 10 minutes. Dextrose 10%: adult 25 g (250 mL),
  child 5 mL/kg; treat glucose below 70 mg/dL. (ADA, NASEMSO)
- Epinephrine anaphylaxis 0.01 mg/kg IM, maximum 0.5 mg of 1 mg/mL (auto-
  injector 0.3 mg; 0.15 mg small child); repeat after 5 minutes. Cardiac
  arrest 1 mg IV/IO of 0.1 mg/mL (10 mL) every 3–5 minutes. IM onset
  5–10 minutes. (AHA, WAO)
- Naloxone 4 mg intranasal spray, or 0.4–2 mg IV/IM/IN titrated to
  breathing; about 1 mL per nostril at most; duration 30–90 minutes.
  (NASEMSO)
- Albuterol 2.5–5 mg nebulised every 20 minutes up to 3 doses. (GINA)
- Amiodarone 300 mg then 150 mg. Adenosine 6 mg then 12 mg. Atropine 1 mg
  every 3–5 minutes, maximum 3 mg; not used in arrest. (AHA 2025)
- Midazolam 10 mg IM over 40 kg or 0.2 mg/kg IN (maximum 10 mg) for a
  seizure lasting 5 minutes or more. (AES 2016)
- Fentanyl 1 mcg/kg (maximum 100 mcg), lasts 30–60 minutes, about 100 times
  morphine's potency; morphine lasts 3–5 hours. Ketamine analgesia
  0.1–0.3 mg/kg. Ondansetron 4 mg. (NASEMSO)
- IV first in cardiac arrest; IO if IV fails (AHA 2025). Cannulas: 14–16 G
  trauma, 18 G most emergencies, 20–22 G routine, 24 G infants. Oral onset
  30 minutes or more.

## Calculations

- 1 g = 1000 mg; 1 mg = 1000 mcg; 1 g = 1,000,000 mcg. Pounds ÷ 2.2 = kg
  (154 lb = 70 kg; 40 lb = 18.2 kg, × 5 mg/kg = 91 mg; 40 ÷ 2.2 ≈ 18 kg).
- Volume = dose ÷ concentration: fentanyl 100 mcg ÷ 50 mcg/mL = 2 mL;
  fentanyl 50 mcg of 100 mcg/2 mL = 1 mL; morphine 4 mg ÷ 10 mg/mL = 0.4 mL;
  ondansetron 4 mg of 4 mg/2 mL = 2 mL; epinephrine 1 mg ÷ 0.1 mg/mL =
  10 mL.
- Midazolam 0.2 mg/kg: 8 kg infant → 1.6 mg ÷ 5 mg/mL = 0.32 mL; 18 kg
  child → 3.6 mg ÷ 5 mg/mL = 0.72 mL.
- Drops/min = volume × drop factor ÷ minutes: 500 mL × 15 ÷ 240 ≈ 31;
  1400 mL (70 kg × 20 mL/kg) × 15 ÷ 60 = 350. Macrodrip 10, 15 or 20
  drops/mL; microdrip 60 drops/mL, where drops/min = mL/hour
  (125 mL/hour = 125 drops/min).

## Safety

- Ratio labels (1:1000, 1:10,000) replaced by mg/mL. Leading zero always
  (0.5 mg), no trailing zero (1 mg, never 1.0 mg). (ISMP, FDA)
- Medicines for opioid use disorder: methadone, buprenorphine, naltrexone;
  alcohol use disorder: disulfiram, acamprosate, naltrexone. (SAMHSA)

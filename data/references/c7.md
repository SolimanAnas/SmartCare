# Chapter 7 – Special Populations: reference notes

The facts the rewritten chapter relies on, each with its source. Every number
on a chapter 7 page or in a right answer must appear here
(`tests/test_content_guides.py`). Sources are international and current:

- **NRP 2025** – AHA / AAP 2025 neonatal resuscitation guideline and the
  Neonatal Resuscitation Program; **ILCOR 2023** – cord management CoSTR.
- **PALS 2025** – AHA pediatric basic and advanced life support guidelines
  and the PALS provider manual (vital-sign tables); **PEPP** – AAP Pediatric
  Education for Prehospital Professionals (Pediatric Assessment Triangle).
- **AAP** – croup, bronchiolitis (2014), febrile seizure and BRUE (2016)
  guidance; **AES 2016** – American Epilepsy Society status epilepticus
  guideline; **GINA** – Global Initiative for Asthma.
- **ACOG** – Practice Bulletins on gestational hypertension and
  pre-eclampsia (222) and postpartum hemorrhage (183); **WHO** –
  recommendations on postpartum hemorrhage (2023) and pre-eclampsia /
  eclampsia (magnesium sulfate regimens).
- **CDC** – sexual assault, HIV post-exposure prophylaxis and IM injection
  guidance; **WHO** – elder abuse fact sheet and BMI classification.
- **TEN-4-FACESp** – bruising clinical decision rule (Pierce et al., 2021).
- **GEMS** – NAEMT Geriatric Education for EMS; **Field Triage 2021** –
  National Guideline for the Field Triage of Injured Patients.
- **AARC** – tracheostomy and airway suctioning guidelines; **DAS** –
  Difficult Airway Society guidance for obese patients; **AHA 2019** – blood
  pressure measurement statement.
- **NREMT / NEMSES** – US National EMS Education Standards (emergency
  childbirth, special populations).

## 7.1 Obstetrics and childbirth

- Imminent birth: crowning, an urge to push, or contractions 2 minutes apart
  or closer: prepare to deliver on scene. (NREMT)
- Warm the room or ambulance to 23–25 °C before birth. (NRP 2025)
- Normal delivery: control the head, check for a nuchal cord (slip it over
  the head; if too tight, clamp twice and cut between), deliver the anterior
  then posterior shoulder, dry and place skin-to-skin. Suction only if the
  airway is blocked, mouth before nose. (NREMT, NRP 2025)
- Vigorous newborn: delay cord clamping at least 60 seconds. (ILCOR 2023,
  NRP 2025) First clamp about 10 cm from the baby, second about 5 cm further
  out; cut between. (NREMT)
- The placenta usually delivers within 30 minutes; never pull on the cord;
  don't delay transport for it. Signs of separation: a gush of blood, the
  cord lengthens, the uterus rises and firms. (NREMT)
- Stages of labour: 1st (cervix dilates fully), 2nd (birth of the baby),
  3rd (placenta); some texts add a 4th (the first hours after birth). (NREMT)
- APGAR at 1 and 5 minutes: appearance, pulse (below 100 = 1, 100 or more
  = 2), grimace, activity, respiration, each scored 0–2; 7–10 is reassuring.
  The APGAR does not decide whether to resuscitate. (NRP 2025)
- Supine hypotension after 20 weeks: the uterus compresses the inferior vena
  cava; turn her to the left side or displace the uterus manually.
  (NREMT, AHA)
- Prolapsed cord: knee-chest position or hips raised, gloved hand lifts the
  presenting part off the cord, keep the cord warm and moist, don't push it
  back, rapid transport. (NREMT)
- Breech: hands off until the body is out to the umbilicus; if the head has
  not delivered after 3 minutes, make an airway with two fingers in a V
  either side of the nose and transport. Limb presentation: knee-chest,
  transport. (NREMT)
- Shoulder dystocia (turtle sign): McRoberts manoeuvre plus suprapubic
  pressure; no hard traction on the head. (ACOG, NREMT)
- Bleeding after 20 weeks: placenta previa (painless, bright red), placental
  abruption (painful, tender rigid uterus, bleeding may be concealed). No
  vaginal examination. (ACOG)
- Pre-eclampsia: BP 140/90 mmHg or more after 20 weeks, and it can appear up
  to 6 weeks after birth. Severe features: 160/110 mmHg or more, headache,
  visual changes, upper abdominal pain, confusion. Eclampsia: seizure.
  (ACOG 222)
- Magnesium sulfate for eclampsia: 4–6 g IV over 20–30 minutes (ACOG), or
  10 g IM (5 g into each buttock) when there is no IV (WHO). Toxicity: loss
  of knee (patellar) reflexes, respiratory rate below 12/min – stop the
  infusion and give calcium gluconate 1 g IV. (ACOG, WHO)
- Postpartum hemorrhage: 1000 mL or more, or any blood loss with signs of
  hypovolemia (ACOG 183; WHO uses 500 mL). Causes: tone (atony, the most
  common), trauma, tissue, thrombin. Fundal massage, breastfeeding, oxytocin
  10 IU IM, tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 minutes within 3 hours of birth
  with a second 1 g after 30 minutes if bleeding continues. Don't pack the
  vagina. (WHO 2023, ACOG)

## 7.2 Gynecologic emergencies

- Ectopic pregnancy: missed period, abdominal or pelvic pain, vaginal
  bleeding; rupture usually 6–8 weeks after the last period; shoulder-tip
  pain (Kehr's sign) and shock mean intra-abdominal blood. Ruptured ectopic
  pregnancy is the leading cause of maternal death in the first trimester.
  Contraception lowers but does not remove the risk. (ACOG)
- Miscarriage: bleeding and cramping before 20 weeks; threatened,
  inevitable, incomplete, complete, missed. Bring passed tissue in a clean
  container. (ACOG)
- Ovarian torsion: sudden severe one-sided pain, nausea and vomiting, may
  come in waves with partial twisting; surgical emergency. Ruptured ovarian
  cyst: sudden pain, often mid-cycle or after sex or exercise; can bleed.
  (ACOG)
- Pelvic inflammatory disease: lower abdominal pain, cervical motion
  tenderness, fever, discharge; usually chlamydia or gonorrhoea; untreated
  it causes infertility and ectopic pregnancy. (CDC STI guidelines)
- Sexual assault: treat injuries first; preserve evidence (no washing,
  changing, eating, drinking, brushing teeth or urinating if possible;
  separate paper bags for clothing); document the patient's words; take the
  patient to a centre with a forensic examiner. HIV post-exposure
  prophylaxis must start within 72 hours; emergency contraception is
  effective up to 120 hours, better the earlier it is given. Adults decide
  about police reporting; abuse of children and vulnerable adults is
  reported according to local law. (CDC, WHO)

## 7.3 Neonatal resuscitation

- About 10% of newborns need help to begin breathing (about 1 in 10); very
  few need compressions or drugs. (NRP 2025)
- Initial steps: warm, dry, stimulate, position the airway, clear secretions
  only if needed. Start PPV within 60 seconds of birth if apnoeic, gasping or
  HR below 100: 40–60 breaths/min, initial pressure 20–25 cmH₂O, up to
  40 cmH₂O if needed. Oxygen: 21% at 35 weeks or more, 21–30% under 35 weeks.
  Pulse oximeter on the right hand. (NRP 2025)
- No chest rise: MR. SOPA – mask, reposition, suction, open mouth, pressure
  increase, alternative airway. (NRP)
- HR below 60 after 30 seconds of ventilation that moves the chest:
  compressions with two thumbs on the lower third of the sternum, one-third
  of the chest depth, 3:1 (90 compressions and 30 breaths per minute),
  oxygen 100%; reassess HR every 60 seconds. Stop compressions when HR is 60
  or more; continue PPV until HR is 100 or more with effective breathing.
  (NRP 2025)
- Epinephrine 0.1 mg/mL: 0.02 mg/kg IV/IO (range 0.01–0.03 mg/kg) every 3–5
  minutes if HR stays below 60; via ET tube 0.1 mg/kg while access is
  obtained. Volume: normal saline 10 mL/kg over 5–10 minutes for suspected
  blood loss. (NRP 2025)
- Pre-ductal SpO₂ targets: 1 minute 60–65%, 2 minutes 65–70%, 3 minutes
  70–75%, 4 minutes 75–80%, 5 minutes 80–85%, 10 minutes 85–95%. Healthy
  newborns take about 10 minutes to reach normal saturation; acrocyanosis is
  normal at first. (NRP)
- Temperature 36.5–37.5 °C. Under 32 weeks: plastic bag up to the neck
  without drying, plus a hat. (NRP 2025)
- ET tube: under 28 weeks or under 1 kg 2.5 mm; 28–34 weeks or 1–2 kg
  3.0 mm; over 34 weeks or over 2 kg 3.5 mm. Depth at the lip: weight in kg
  + 6 cm. Size 1 laryngeal mask from 34 weeks or 2 kg. (NRP)
- Not recommended: routine oral, nasal or tracheal suctioning (including for
  meconium); naloxone during resuscitation; 100% oxygen to start
  ventilation in term babies. (NRP 2025)
- Fetal shunts: ductus venosus, foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus. (NRP)

## 7.4 Pediatric assessment and emergencies

- Pediatric Assessment Triangle: appearance (TICLS), work of breathing,
  circulation to skin; done hands-off from the doorway. Impressions:
  respiratory distress, respiratory failure, compensated or decompensated
  shock, brain or metabolic dysfunction, cardiopulmonary failure. (PEPP)
- Normal heart rate (awake) and respiratory rate: newborn (to 28 days)
  100–205 and 30–53; infant (to 1 year) 100–180 and 30–53; toddler
  (1–2 years) 98–140 and 22–37; preschool (3–5 years) 80–120 and 20–28;
  school age (6–11 years) 75–118 and 18–25; adolescent (12–15 years) 60–100
  and 12–20. (PALS)
- Hypotension: term newborns (0–28 days) systolic below 60 mmHg; infants
  (1–12 months) below 70 mmHg; children 1–10 years below 70 + (2 × age in
  years), e.g. below 78 mmHg at 4 years; over 10 years below 90 mmHg.
  Hypotension is a late sign. (PALS)
- Length-based tape estimates weight up to about 36 kg. (Broselow)
- Croup: barking cough, stridor, hoarseness, over days. Nebulized
  epinephrine 0.5 mL/kg of 1 mg/mL (maximum 5 mL) for stridor at rest;
  dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg (maximum 16 mg). Epiglottitis: rapid onset, high
  fever, drooling, tripod, muffled voice; don't examine the throat. (AAP)
- Asthma: albuterol 2.5–5 mg nebulized or 4–8 puffs by spacer; ipratropium
  250–500 mcg nebulized. (GINA, PALS)
- Bronchiolitis: under 2 years; nasal suction; oxygen if SpO₂ is below 90%;
  albuterol not routinely recommended. (AAP 2014)
- Febrile seizure: 6 months to 5 years. Treat a seizure lasting 5 minutes
  or more. (AAP, AES 2016)
- BRUE: infant under 1 year, event under 1 minute, resolved. (AAP 2016)
- SVT: 220/min or more in infants, 180/min or more in children. Adenosine
  0.1 mg/kg rapid push (maximum 6 mg), then 0.2 mg/kg (maximum 12 mg);
  synchronized cardioversion 0.5–1 J/kg, then 2 J/kg. (PALS)
- Shock: capillary refill over 2 seconds; isotonic bolus 10–20 mL/kg over
  5–20 minutes with reassessment, 10 mL/kg if heart disease is possible.
  Glucose below 60 mg/dL: dextrose 10% 5 mL/kg. (PALS)
- Choking: over 1 year abdominal thrusts; under 1 year 5 back blows and 5
  chest thrusts. (PALS 2025)
- Pediatric CPR: 30:2 alone, 15:2 with two rescuers, 100–120/min; depth
  about 4 cm in infants and about 5 cm in children. Infants: two thumbs
  encircling or the heel of one hand; the two-finger technique is no longer
  recommended. Epinephrine 0.01 mg/kg IV/IO (maximum 1 mg) every 3–5
  minutes. Defibrillation 2 J/kg, then 4 J/kg; later shocks up to 10 J/kg or
  the adult dose. (PALS 2025)

## 7.5 Child abuse and neglect

- TEN-4-FACESp: bruising on the torso, ears or neck in a child under
  4 years; any bruise in an infant 4 months or younger; frenulum, angle of
  jaw, cheeks, eyelids, subconjunctival; patterned bruising. (Pierce et al.,
  2021) A baby of 2 weeks cannot roll off a couch. (AAP)
- Bruise colour cannot reliably date a bruise. (AAP)
- Abusive head trauma: most common under 1 year; subdural hemorrhage,
  retinal hemorrhages, brain swelling, often no external marks. (AAP)
- Fractures suspicious for abuse: posterior rib, classic metaphyseal
  lesions, long-bone fractures in non-ambulatory children, multiple
  fractures of different ages. (AAP)
- Reporting needs reasonable suspicion, not proof; good-faith reports are
  protected in most jurisdictions. (AAP, NREMT)
- Safe sleep: on the back, own flat cot, no soft bedding. (AAP)

## 7.6 Geriatrics

- Older adults (65 or older) often present atypically: MI without chest
  pain, sepsis without fever. Delirium: acute, fluctuating attention; the 4AT
  is a validated screen. (GEMS)
- Long lie: on the floor for 1 hour or more. (GEMS)
- Field Triage 2021: age 65 or older with systolic below 110 mmHg is
  high-risk; anticoagulant use is a moderate-risk factor.
- Polypharmacy: 5 or more medicines. (GEMS, AGS Beers criteria)
- Elder abuse: about 1 in 6 people aged 60 or older experienced abuse in
  community settings in the past year. (WHO)
- Hip fracture: shortened, externally rotated leg; no traction splint.
  (GEMS, PHTLS)
- DNR orders cover CPR only; POLST / MOLST and local equivalents (ReSPECT)
  set wider treatment levels. (GEMS)

## 7.7 Patients with special challenges

- Use qualified interpreters, not family or children. (NREMT)
- Atlantoaxial instability occurs in a minority of people with Down
  syndrome. (AAP)
- Tracheostomy problems: DOPE (displacement, obstruction, pneumothorax,
  equipment); suction to the pre-measured depth for under 15 seconds; a
  replacement tube of the same size or one size smaller. (AARC)
- Lithium toxicity: vomiting, diarrhoea, tremor, ataxia, confusion;
  dehydration, NSAIDs, diuretics and ACE inhibitors raise levels.
- Capacity: understand, retain, weigh information, communicate a choice.
- Hyperkalemia after missed dialysis: peaked T waves, then wide QRS.

## 7.8 Bariatric considerations

- BMI = weight in kg ÷ height in metres squared; obesity 30 or more; class
  III (severe) 40 or more. (WHO)
- Ramp so the ear canal is level with the sternal notch; head up 25–30°;
  pre-oxygenate for at least 3 minutes; nasal oxygen at 15 L/min during
  intubation attempts. (DAS)
- BP cuff bladder width about 40% and length about 80% of the arm
  circumference; small cuffs read falsely high. (AHA 2019)
- IO: 45 mm needle for large patients. IM: 38 mm (1.5 inch) or longer
  needle. (Manufacturer guidance, CDC)
- Moving: 4–6 or more people, transfer aids, never exceed equipment
  ratings. (NREMT)
- CPR and defibrillation unchanged; rotate compressors every 2 minutes.
  (AHA 2025)
- Glucagon 1 mg IM for hypoglycaemia without IV access. (AHA)

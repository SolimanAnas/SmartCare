## 8.1 Heat-related illness

- Heat stroke: core temperature usually above 40 °C (104 °F) with CNS
  dysfunction (confusion, seizures, coma). Heat exhaustion: normal mental
  status, core under 40 °C. Heat cramps: painful spasms. (WMS)
- Exertional heat stroke: cold-water immersion is the fastest cooling; cool
  first, transport second; aim to get below 39 °C within 30 minutes of
  collapse; stop active cooling at about 39 °C (102 °F) to avoid
  overshoot. Rectal temperature is the reliable field core measure.
  (ACSM, NATA, WMS)
- Classic heat stroke: evaporative cooling, ice packs, cold IV fluids. No
  antipyretics; no alcohol rubs. (WMS)
- Exercise-associated hyponatremia: confusion, vomiting, normal or mildly
  raised temperature (e.g. 38.3 °C) after drinking heavily; no free water.
  (WMS)

## 8.2 Cold exposure and hypothermia

- Swiss staging (ERC 2025, WMS): mild 32–35 °C (alert, shivering);
  moderate 28–32 °C (drowsy, shivering stops); severe below 28 °C
  (unconscious); cardiac arrest risk rises below 28 °C and arrest is often
  below 24 °C.
- Check for breathing and pulse for up to 1 minute. Below 30 °C: up to 3
  shocks, then hold until above 30 °C; withhold epinephrine below 30 °C and
  double the interval (every 6–10 minutes) at 30–35 °C. AHA allows standard
  defibrillation alongside rewarming. Transport arrests to an ECLS centre.
  (ERC 2025, AHA 2025)
- Do not start or stop if: obvious lethal injury, frozen chest, avalanche
  burial over 60 minutes with an obstructed airway. (ERC 2025)
- Warm IV fluids 38–42 °C. Frostbite: thaw only if no refreezing risk, in
  water at 37–39 °C for 15–30 minutes; ibuprofen. (WMS)

## 8.3 Drowning and submersion

- Drowning is respiratory impairment from submersion or immersion; "dry",
  "wet" and "secondary" drowning are not recommended terms. (WHO, WMS)
- Start with 5 rescue breaths (ERC 2025), then 30:2 (adults) or 15:2
  (children, two rescuers). Pediatric rescue breathing 1 breath every
  2–3 seconds (20–30 breaths/min); adult 1 every 6 seconds. (ERC 2025,
  AHA 2025)
- No abdominal thrusts. Spinal precautions only with a trauma mechanism.
  Hospital for anyone needing breaths or CPR or with cough, foam, abnormal
  lung sounds, SpO₂ below 95% or altered mental status. (WMS, ERC)

## 8.4 Diving and altitude

- Arterial gas embolism: within minutes of surfacing, usually under 10
  minutes. Decompression sickness: usually within 6 hours, up to 24 hours.
  100% oxygen; lie flat (head-down or left-side positions are no longer
  recommended); fluids; hyperbaric recompression; fly low or pressurised.
  (DAN, UHMS)
- AMS 6–12 hours after ascent; Lake Louise Score: headache plus total of 3
  or more, each symptom 0–3. HACE: descend, oxygen, dexamethasone 8 mg then
  4 mg every 6 hours. HAPE: descend, oxygen, nifedipine ER 30 mg every
  12 hours. Descend 300–1000 m or until symptoms resolve. (WMS 2024)

## 8.5 Bites, stings and envenomation

- Anaphylaxis: epinephrine IM 0.01 mg/kg, maximum 0.5 mg (auto-injector
  0.3 mg; 0.15 mg for small children); repeat after 5 minutes. (WAO, ERC)
- Snakebite: immobilise at about heart level, mark the swelling edge every
  15 minutes, no cutting, sucking, tourniquet, ice or shock. Pressure
  immobilisation for neurotoxic elapids where advised (Australia). (WMS,
  WHO, ARC)
- Bee stings: remove quickly by any method. Hot water about 45 °C for
  30–90 minutes for stingray and venomous fish; vinegar for box jellyfish.
  (WMS)

## 8.6 Hazardous materials and radiation

- Approach upwind, uphill, upstream; isolate at least 100 m (330 feet) in
  all directions until the ERG gives a distance. (ERG 2024)
- Placard colours: orange explosives (class 1); red flammable gas or liquid
  (2.1, 3); green non-flammable gas (2.2); white with skull toxic gas or
  poison (2.3, 6); red and white stripes flammable solid (4.1); yellow
  oxidiser (5); yellow and white radioactive (7); black and white corrosive
  (8). (US DOT, UN)
- PPE levels A, B, C, D. (OSHA, EPA)
- Removing clothing removes up to 90% of contamination. (CHEMM, REMM)
- Hydroxocobalamin 5 g IV over 15 minutes (child 70 mg/kg). (CHEMM)
- Time to vomiting after radiation: under 10 minutes – over 8 Gy, likely
  fatal; 1–2 hours – about 2–4 Gy; after 2 hours or none – under 2 Gy.
  (REMM)

## 8.7 CBRNE and terrorism

- Nerve agents: atropine 2–6 mg IM/IV, repeat every 5 minutes until
  secretions dry; pralidoxime; benzodiazepine for seizures. Auto-injectors
  (DuoDote) contain atropine 2.1 mg and pralidoxime 600 mg. (CHEMM)
- Phosgene: delayed lung oedema; mustard: delayed blistering. Anthrax is not
  spread person to person; pneumonic plague by droplets; smallpox airborne
  and contact, centrifugal synchronous rash. (CDC)

## 8.8 Mass-casualty incidents and triage

- START: walking green; apnoeic after airway opening black; red if
  respiratory rate above 30/min, no radial pulse or capillary refill over
  2 seconds, or can't follow commands. (START)
- JumpSTART: children under about 8 years; 5 rescue breaths for apnoea with
  a pulse; red if respiratory rate below 15 or above 45/min, no palpable
  pulse, or P/U on AVPU. (Romig)

## 8.9 Incident Command System

- Span of control 3–7, about 5. Command Staff: Safety, Public Information,
  Liaison (3). General Staff: Operations, Planning, Logistics,
  Finance/Administration (4); Intelligence/Investigations optional.
  (NIMS 2017)
